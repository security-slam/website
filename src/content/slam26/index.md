Whether you're a maintainer, a contributor, or an end user cheering on the projects you depend on, getting up to speed with the Security Slam is a quick process.

## Let's get to know the flow

This is not a traditional hackathon. There are no teams, only shared objectives. There are no presentations or scoring, only submission and evaluation. There are no prizes, only recognitions.

- Individuals who register may contribute in any way they see fit.
- Maintainers who register within the first week may request to have their project added to the [Participating Projects](/slam26/participating-projects) page.
- Maintainers submit completion notifications from the relevant badge page. Everyone included on one qualifies for a digital badge and physical pin issued by our sponsors.
- Projects that complete all objectives qualify for a recognition on this website and a physical plaque.

This leads us to the next obvious question...

## What are the objectives?

The Slam has six. Five center around the _Open Source Project Security Baseline_, created by and for maintainers within the [OpenSSF](https://openssf.org). The sixth covers voluntary EU Cyber Resilience Act readiness.

1. **Start here:** [The Cleaner](/library/cleaner) publishes a `security-insights.yml` that accurately describes how your project handles security today. You'll keep adding to it as you complete the other badges.
2. [The Chronicler](/library/chronicler) writes the documentation the Baseline expects at your project's [maturity level](/library/baseline-levels).
3. [The Inspector](/library/inspector) documents what your project does and how it could be attacked.
4. [The Mechanizer](/library/mechanizer) wires a recurring Baseline scan into your default branch. A passing score is not required.
5. [The Defender](/library/defender) reaches a passing Baseline status for your maturity level, published on grc.store. The four badges above are stepping stones toward this one.
6. [CRA Readiness](/library/cra-readiness) implements the CRA guidelines provided by your project's steward. Details will be released on October 12th.

Now based on who you are, your first steps may be different...

## So where should you begin?

[Register now](/slam26/register) if you haven't already. A project is eligible for recognitions once a maintainer signs up and a Slam Organizer confirms the registration. Individuals need to register personally and contribute to a confirmed project.

Once that's sorted, coordinate with other contributors through official project channels, such as GitHub Issues and Slack. Contributions are less likely to be accepted if maintainers are surprised by them... and it'd be a tragedy to duplicate effort that someone else has already started.

## What if you have questions?

Slam Advisors are standing by to collaborate, speculate, give opinions, and share their experience. These are community members who have already been through the tasks you're completing now.
